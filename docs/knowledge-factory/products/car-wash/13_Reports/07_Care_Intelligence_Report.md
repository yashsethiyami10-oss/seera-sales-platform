# MUV Car Wash™ — Care Intelligence Report

> Truth → Safety → Care → Clarity → Actionability → Validation, applied throughout.

## Where this discipline mattered most

- **KO-CW-CONV-008 (Claims Inquiry)** — the highest-risk conversation in this package. Truth
  first: no wax, gloss-lock, or paint-protection claim is sourced. Never hedges toward "it
  probably does" — this is the clearest test case this session of resisting an unsourced claim
  that exists, in plain sight, on a different but adjacently-named real product (MUV Shield).
- **KO-CW-CONV-007 (Compatibility Inquiry)** — honest disclosure of a real, total content gap
  (vehicle-surface compatibility) rather than a reassuring guess.
- **KO-CW-CONV-004/006/011 (Usage/Safety/Storage referenced via CMS)** — a new form of honesty
  discipline: correctly citing the architecturally-correct reference pattern while still
  disclosing, every time, that the underlying source is currently empty.

## Never Invent discipline, extended to Claims Validation

This package tested the discipline against a real, tempting case: an adjacently-named, already-
live product (MUV Shield) using exactly the claim language ("gloss-lock," "wax") that would be
easy to assume applies here too. It held the line throughout `02_Product_Architecture.md`,
`03_Product_Intelligence.md`, `05_Customer_Conversation.md`, and `07_Objection_Handling.md`.

## Comparison to Hand Wash

Hand Wash's highest-discipline case was an unverified antibacterial claim implied by a product's
own name (Life Shield). Car Wash extends the same discipline to a claim implied by a *different*
product's real, live marketing copy — arguably an easier trap to fall into, since the claim
language already exists somewhere real in the codebase, not just as a name-based inference.
