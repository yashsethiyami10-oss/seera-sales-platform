## KO-SC-CH2-001 — Sales Process & Lead Generation

- **Purpose:** Give the eleven-step sales journey framework and the ten lead sources, with the
  pipeline-discipline rule.
- **Scope:** Sales Process (Figure 2.1), Lead Generation, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VIII, Ch.37 (Sales Process, Lead Generation).
- **Outputs:** Figure 2.1 eleven-step framework; ten lead sources.
- **Dependencies:** None (chapter-opening KO).
- **Relationships:** governs KO-SC-CH2-002 through 014.
- **Governance Rules:** *"A list is not a pipeline. Every lead requires source, relevance,
  owner, status, and next action."*
- **Validation Rules:** Figure 2.1's eleven steps preserved in exact order (including the
  source's own lowercase "qualify" in step 3, reproduced verbatim, not corrected); all ten lead
  sources preserved.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content — Figure 2.1 — Sales Journey Framework:** 1. Identify a relevant market or account.
2. Generate or receive a lead. 3. qualify fit, authority, need, timing, and economics. 4.
Understand the customer or partner context. 5. Present the relevant Muv value. 6. Demonstrate
or provide proof where appropriate. 7. Clarify questions and objections. 8. Agree commercial
and operational terms. 9. Confirm order, payment, supply, and responsibilities. 10. Fulfil and
support. 11. Follow up and learn.

**Lead Generation — leads may come from:** marketing; website or marketplace; field
prospecting; retailer/distributor research; institutional lists; referrals; trade/community
networks; events; customer expansion; creator or campaign response. A list is not a pipeline.
Every lead requires source, relevance, owner, status, and next action.

## KO-SC-CH2-015 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VIII, Ch.37, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 11-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-SC-CH2-001 through 014.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 11 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Treating all leads as equal. Presenting before understanding. Pressuring
customers who lack fit. Leaving commercial promises outside the CRM. Appointing partners only
for reach. Shipping opening stock without sell-through support. Expanding before supply and
service readiness.

**Best Practices:** Qualify early and respectfully. Make next steps explicit. Record repeated
objections. Align sales commitments with inventory and manufacturing. Train partners before
activation. Pilot territories with scale and stop criteria. Review partner sell-through, not
only sell-in.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Lead source, fit, owner, and stage recorded.
- [ ] Customer/partner need and authority understood.
- [ ] Product value and demonstration approved.
- [ ] Objections and open questions documented.
- [ ] Price, terms, supply, and service authorised.
- [ ] Order and follow-up controlled.
- [ ] Channel selection supported by economics and capability.
- [ ] Partner due diligence and onboarding complete.
- [ ] Territory and conflict rules clear.
- [ ] Pilot market has metrics and decision conditions.
- [ ] Sales and partner training verified.

**Key Takeaways:** Sales helps relevant buyers decide; it does not force fit. Distribution is a
designed route-to-market system. Partner reach matters only when economics, operations, trust,
and sell-through align. Expansion should move through controlled local learning.

**Chapter Summary:** *"Sales & Distribution converts market interest into qualified
conversations, authorised transactions, reliable channel execution, and disciplined
expansion."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: Marketplace Operations governs platform
setup, listings, inventory, orders, service, settlements, and operational reliability"* — the
