## KO-SC-CH1-001 — Sales Philosophy & Customer Value before Transaction

- **Purpose:** Establish the five-stage sales-value chain, the seven-stage commercial system,
  and the six-buyer-type value-question table, with the Founder Note governing ambition vs.
  evidence.
- **Scope:** Sales Philosophy (Figure 1.1), Customer Value before Transaction, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VIII, Ch.36 (Sales Philosophy, Customer Value before
  Transaction).
- **Outputs:** Five-stage value chain; Figure 1.1 seven-stage commercial system; six-item
  never-change list; six-row buyer-value table; Founder Note.
- **Dependencies:** None (chapter-opening KO).
- **Relationships:** governs KO-SC-CH1-002 through 008.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Sales must not change product truth to close a transaction."*
  Founder Note: *"Ambition must be preserved while targets, budgets, prices, and projections
  remain tied to their evidence and approval state."*
- **Validation Rules:** Figure 1.1's seven stages preserved in exact order; all six buyer rows
  preserved; the Founder Note never dropped.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Sales connects: Verified product value → Relevant customer or partner need → Clear
commercial choice → Reliable fulfilment → Continued relationship.

**Figure 1.1 — Muv Commercial System:** Product truth → Marketing understanding → Sales
conversation → Channel transaction → Distribution execution → Customer experience → Revenue and
learning.

Sales must not change product truth to close a transaction. It should help the buyer
understand: what the product is; whether it is relevant; what value it creates; what it costs;
how it will be supplied; what conditions and support apply.

**Customer Value before Transaction — a transaction is valuable only when the customer or
partner receives a supportable outcome:**

| Buyer | Value question |
|---|---|
| Consumer | Does the product solve the intended problem at acceptable value and effort? |
| Retailer | Will the product be understandable, available, sellable, and supportable? |
| Distributor | Can the portfolio, territory, margin structure, stock, and service operate sustainably? |
| Institution | Does product performance, use, supply, documentation, and economics meet the use case? |
| Marketplace customer | Is the listing accurate, trustworthy, available, and easy to evaluate? |
| Franchise/partner | Are responsibilities, economics, support, and risk clearly understood? |

> **Founder Note** — The recovered record contains ambitious early sales targets, portfolio
> breadth, launch-budget thinking, price-list work, institutional pitches, and channel
> questions. Ambition must be preserved while targets, budgets, prices, and projections remain
> tied to their evidence and approval state.

**Figure 1.2 — Price Architecture:**

| Layer | Purpose |
|---|---|

## KO-SC-CH1-009 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VIII, Ch.36, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 9-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-SC-CH1-001 through 008.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 9 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Pricing only by competitor observation. Applying one margin logic to every
channel. Confusing MRP with net realised revenue. Ignoring return, fulfilment, and promotional
cost. Giving schemes verbally without scope or expiry. Changing online price without updating
the commercial system. Treating revenue target as proof of demand.

**Best Practices:** Use one controlled SKU and price master. Date every cost basis. Model full
channel contribution. Protect customer value and partner viability together. Review realised
versus planned economics. Preserve price and promotion history.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Exact product, pack, and SKU identified.
- [ ] Complete cost basis dated and verified.
- [ ] Customer value assessed.
- [ ] Channel economics modelled.
- [ ] MRP and transaction rules aligned.
- [ ] Tax, fees, returns, credit, and promotion considered.
- [ ] Price list version and effective date controlled.
- [ ] Pack, listing, invoice, and partner records aligned.
- [ ] Approval and review trigger recorded.

**Key Takeaways:** Sales begins with real value, not pressure. Price architecture connects
cost, customer value, channel economics, and long-term viability. MRP, transaction price, and
promotion are different commercial controls. Revenue quality matters more than billing volume
alone.
