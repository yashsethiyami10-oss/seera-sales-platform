## KO-CI-CH2-001 — Product Experience, Product Truth & Choice Architecture

- **Purpose:** Give the nine-stage product experience chain, the technical-truth deferral, and
  the choice-explanation discipline.
- **Scope:** Product Experience (Figure 2.1), Product Truth in Experience, Choice Architecture.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.42 (Product Experience, Product Truth in
  Experience, Choice Architecture).
- **Outputs:** The experience chain; eight-row expectation table; five never-do rules; eight
  choice-explanation elements.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH1-001 (Customer Experience Philosophy).
- **Relationships:** *"Volume V and Volume VI own technical product truth and quality"* —
  matches Domain 2's own Volume V/VI deferral pattern (`KO-PM-CH1-006`), cited not restated.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Never: recommend the wrong product to close a sale; conceal usage
  limitations; show a pack or presentation unlike the delivered product; promise a performance or
  time result without evidence; treat customer misuse as the first explanation without
  listening."*
- **Validation Rules:** All five never-do rules preserved together; the eight-row table never
  presented partially.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** The customer experiences a product as one system: **Need → Selection →
Expectation → Purchase → Receipt → Opening/presentation → Use/consumption → Result → Memory.**

**Figure 2.1 — Product Experience Chain:**

| Experience area | Customer expectation |
|---|---|
| Product choice | Understandable category, variant, size, and suitability |
| Information | Accurate benefits, use, price, ingredients/details where applicable |
| Availability | Product shown can be obtained |
| Receipt | Correct, intact, clean, and identifiable item |
| First impression | Pack/product/presentation matches expectation |
| Use/consumption | Directions, format, performance, sensory experience |
| Value | Outcome and experience justify total cost and effort |
| Support | Questions and problems have a clear route |

**Product Truth in Experience:** Volume V and Volume VI own technical product truth and
quality. CX ensures that truth is correctly understood and experienced. **Never:** recommend the
wrong product to close a sale; conceal usage limitations; show a pack or presentation unlike the
delivered product; promise a performance or time result without evidence; treat customer misuse
as the first explanation without listening.

**Choice Architecture — help customers choose by explaining:** category/purpose; primary
approved value; variant/fragrance/flavour where relevant; size or portion; use; important
differences; price and value; support. Too many options without structure increase effort and
wrong selection.

## KO-CI-CH2-008 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.42, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 10-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH2-001 through 007.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 10 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Treating the product alone as the product experience. Showing unavailable
menu/catalogue items. Giving inconsistent instructions by channel. Failing to record batch/order
context. Labelling every negative experience "customer preference." Sending retention promotions
before resolving first-use issues.

**Best Practices:** Test the journey using the actual current product. Review choice
architecture with real customer questions. Include first-use guidance in launch readiness.
Connect delivery and product-quality evidence. Monitor repeat by cohort and product. Close the
feedback loop with product and manufacturing.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Product and customer use case aligned.
- [ ] Choice information clear and current.
- [ ] Price, size, product, and availability consistent.
- [ ] Purchase and delivery expectations visible.
- [ ] Order/product accuracy checks operate.
- [ ] First-use guidance approved.
- [ ] Support route easy to find.
- [ ] Product-quality signals linked to batch/order.
- [ ] Repeat and retention reflect real value.
- [ ] Technical issues routed to owning systems.

**Key Takeaways:** The product experience includes selection, purchase, delivery, use, value,
and support. First use is a major trust moment. Food-service and product contexts both require
clarity, consistency, and recovery. Repeat purchase is earned by delivered value, not reminders
alone.

complete practical and sensory reality. It protects correct choice, delivery, first use,
consistency, and repeat."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: Outlet Culture & Environment
governs service philosophy, support channels, communication standards, team behaviour, and
physical/digital environments"* — the sourced justification for Chapter 3 following next.
