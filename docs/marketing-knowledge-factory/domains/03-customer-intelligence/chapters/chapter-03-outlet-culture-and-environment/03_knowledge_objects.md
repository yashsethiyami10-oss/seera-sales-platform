## KO-CI-CH3-001 — Customer Support Philosophy & Culture before Script

- **Purpose:** Establish the nine-stage service system, the nine service-quality dimensions,
  and the eight culture requirements a script cannot substitute for.
- **Scope:** Customer Support Philosophy (Figure 3.1), Culture before Script, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.43 (Customer Support Philosophy, Culture
  before Script).
- **Outputs:** Figure 3.1 nine-stage flow; nine quality dimensions; eight culture requirements;
  the Customer Experience Rule.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH1-001, KO-CI-CH2-007.
- **Relationships:** governs KO-CI-CH3-002 through 008.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Never make the customer carry Muv's internal coordination
  problem."*
- **Validation Rules:** Figure 3.1's nine-stage sequence preserved in exact order; all nine
  quality dimensions and eight culture requirements preserved together.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Support exists to help the customer move from uncertainty or difficulty toward
clarity, safety, resolution, and trust.

**Figure 3.1 — Customer Service System:** Receive → Listen → Identify → Understand → Respond →
Resolve or escalate → Confirm → Record → Learn.

Service quality is not only speed. It includes: accessibility; accuracy; empathy; ownership;
clarity; fairness; continuity; resolution; learning.

**Culture before Script:** A script cannot repair a culture that avoids ownership. Service
culture requires: respect for every customer; calm listening; no blame; truthful limits;
permission to escalate; cross-functional cooperation; accurate records; leadership attention to
repeated friction.

> **Customer Experience Rule** — Never make the customer carry Muv's internal coordination
> problem.

## KO-CI-CH3-009 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.43, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 12-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH3-001 through 008.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 12 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Measuring service only by first response. Using scripts before
understanding the issue. Asking customers to repeat information. Treating social messages as
non-service. Escalating without context. Closing a case when transferred. Leaving physical/
digital environment problems outside CX ownership.

**Best Practices:** Use one case identity across channels. Maintain an approved knowledge base.
Train for normal and high-risk scenarios. Give teams clear escalation and recovery authority.
Review case quality, not only volume. Observe the actual customer environment.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Support philosophy and ownership clear.
- [ ] Channels have accountable owners.
- [ ] Customer identity/context verified appropriately.
- [ ] Listening and classification complete.
- [ ] Answer uses current approved knowledge.
- [ ] Safety and quality triggers active.
- [ ] Escalation includes full context.
- [ ] Customer updates and next steps clear.
- [ ] Resolution confirmed.
- [ ] Case and learning recorded.
- [ ] Teams trained and assessed.
- [ ] Physical/digital environment reviewed.

**Key Takeaways:** Service combines accuracy, empathy, ownership, and resolution. Culture
determines whether scripts create trust. Every support channel belongs to one connected service
system. Escalation must protect the customer before the metric.
