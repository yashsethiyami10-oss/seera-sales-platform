## KO-CI-CH4-001 — Complaint Philosophy & Listening before Responding

- **Purpose:** Establish what a complaint is and is not, and the seven listening questions
  that must precede any response.
- **Scope:** Complaint Philosophy, Listening before Responding, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.44 (Complaint Philosophy, Listening before
  Responding).
- **Outputs:** Four-part complaint definition; seven listening questions; the "no defence
  first" rule.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH1-001, KO-CI-CH3-004.
- **Relationships:** governs KO-CI-CH4-002 through 008.
- **Governance Rules:** *"It is not automatically proof of technical cause. It must be
  respected and verified."* *"Do not begin with defence, policy, or blame."*
- **Validation Rules:** All four definition elements and all seven questions preserved
  together.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** A complaint is: a customer experience requiring response; evidence of possible
failure or expectation mismatch; an opportunity to restore trust; a source of organisational
learning. It is not automatically proof of technical cause. It must be respected and verified.

**Listening before Responding — begin with:** 1. What happened? 2. What did the customer
expect? 3. What was the impact? 4. Which product/order/channel/location was involved? 5. Is
there a safety or urgent risk? 6. What evidence is available? 7. What outcome is the customer
seeking? Do not begin with defence, policy, or blame.

|---|---|

appropriate apology; practical action; reliable updates; fair resolution; confirmation;
prevention. An apology without action can feel empty. Action without acknowledgement can feel
mechanical.

> **Customer Experience Rule** — Recovery must never require the customer to prove the same
> verified fact repeatedly.

## KO-CI-CH4-009 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IX, Ch.44, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 11-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CI-CH4-001 through 008.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 11 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Responding before listening. Treating every complaint as customer misuse.
Offering compensation without investigation or learning. Closing after refund while root cause
remains. Using ratings alone as insight. Converting loud feedback into universal policy.
Failing to tell the customer what happens next.

**Best Practices:** Preserve exact customer wording. Use severity and ownership rules. Link
cases to products, orders, batches, and channels. Communicate knowns and unknowns. Review
recurring themes cross-functionally. Verify that prevention worked.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Complaint acknowledged and listened to.
- [ ] Safety/urgency assessed.
- [ ] Exact product/order/channel context captured.
- [ ] Evidence preserved.
- [ ] Customer statement separated from technical conclusion.
- [ ] Resolution authority and next step clear.
- [ ] Customer updated.
- [ ] Resolution confirmed.
- [ ] Root cause and owner recorded where required.
- [ ] Learning routed across functions.
- [ ] Recurrence prevention verified.

**Key Takeaways:** Complaints are customer outcomes and evidence. Recovery begins with
listening and protection. Fair resolution and root-cause prevention are separate obligations.
Feedback becomes a rule only after validation and authority.
