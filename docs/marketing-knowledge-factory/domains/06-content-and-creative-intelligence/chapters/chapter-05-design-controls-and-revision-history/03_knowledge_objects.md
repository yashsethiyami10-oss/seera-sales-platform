## KO-CC-CH5-008 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Future Scalability, Final Design Approval
  Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IV, Ch.20, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 8-item Future Scalability list; 12-item Final
  Design Approval Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways; Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CC-CH5-001 through 007.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern; transitions out of Part IV.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 12 checklist items in original order; the source's own "Final
  Design Approval Checklist" title preserved.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Treating a revision request as licence to redesign the whole asset.
Skipping the brief and working from memory of a past version. Publishing before Founder or
authority approval. Losing track of which version is current. Allowing vendors to work from
outdated references. Failing to archive superseded assets. Auditing inconsistently or not at
all.

**Best Practices:** Always start from a written brief. Track every revision with a reason.
Maintain one central, access-controlled asset library. Hold external vendors to the same
standard as internal designers. Run the Design Audit on a regular cadence, not only when a
problem is noticed. Treat Founder approval as the final gate, not a formality. Archive, never
delete, superseded work.

**Future Scalability:** As the design volume grows, expect: more designers and vendors working
in parallel; more channels requiring simultaneous consistency; more revision cycles running
concurrently; a growing need for a searchable central asset library; a growing need for
role-based access control; potential need for a dedicated design-operations function; growing
importance of the Design Audit as a scaling mechanism; growing reliance on AI-assisted
production requiring the same governance discipline at higher volume.

**Final Design Approval Checklist:**
- [ ] Brief is complete and approved before work begins.
- [ ] Concept aligns with the brief's key message and mandatory elements.
- [ ] Internal design review completed.
- [ ] Founder or authority review completed.
- [ ] All revision requests stayed within the brief's original scope.
- [ ] Final version is clearly marked and distinguished from prior versions.
- [ ] Asset filed in the central, version-controlled library.
- [ ] Asset meets the format and quality standard for its channel.
- [ ] Mandatory information is present and accurate.
- [ ] No unapproved claims are present.
- [ ] Vendor or external designer work meets internal standard.
- [ ] Asset has passed the Design Audit for its channel.

**Key Takeaways:** Design work moves through a defined, repeatable workflow, not an ad hoc
process. Revision exists to refine within scope, not to reopen the whole concept. Asset
management and the Design Audit are what keep consistency intact as volume grows. Founder or
authority approval remains the final gate at every stage of scale.

**Chapter Summary:** *"Design Controls & Revision History exists to make sure creative
quality does not depend on memory or goodwill — it depends on a defined, auditable system that
holds as the team and channel count grow."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: Part V — MUV
Product Sutra™"* — noted for completeness; Part V is out of scope for Domain 6.

**Content — MUV Design System in One View:**

| Chapter | Core Governing Idea |
|---|---|
| 1. Packaging Architecture | Premium effects must never obscure logo, product type, variant, or mandatory information |
| 2. Label Information System | Compliance authority confirms presence, wording, and legal form of mandatory information; design cannot invent compliance |
| 3. Visual Asset Production | Never change the bottle, label, size, or height; visual enhancement supports truth, never replaces it |
| 4. 3D Visualization & Mockups | A mockup communicates an idea; it never certifies a fact |
| 5. Design Controls & Revision History | A revision refines within scope; it is never permission to redesign |
| Category Differentiation | Category colour direction distinguishes product lines without breaking brand unity |
| Digital Design System | One shared system of components keeps web, social, and app experiences coherent |
| AI-Generated Asset Governance (packaging/photography) | AI may assist production; it cannot become the source of product truth |
| AI-Generated Asset Governance (3D/mockup/UI) | Every generated 3D, mockup, or UI asset answers to the same standard as a photograph |
| Design Workflow | Ten defined stages, not an ad hoc process |
| Asset Management | One central, version-controlled, access-controlled library |
| Design Audit | Ten channels, ten checks, run on a regular cadence |

**Master Design Principles:** 1. The real, approved product is the single source of truth. 2.
Compliance content is confirmed by authority, never invented by design. 3. Premium execution
never obscures required clarity. 4. Concept and final are never interchangeable. 5. Every
generated or rendered asset is verified against a truth reference before use. 6. One shared
design system holds every digital surface together. 7. Revision refines; it does not reopen.
8. Every asset is version-controlled and traceable. 9. External vendors meet the same standard
as internal designers. 10. Consistency is maintained by a recurring audit, not by memory.

**Master Design Checklist:**
- [ ] Product truth preserved in every packaging and visual asset.
- [ ] Mandatory information confirmed by compliance authority, not invented.
- [ ] Premium effects tested against the "harder to understand" rule.
- [ ] Category differentiation applied without breaking brand unity.
- [ ] Every AI-generated asset passed its applicable governance protocol.
- [ ] Mockups and renders never used as the sole source of product facts.
- [ ] Digital Design System components used consistently across web, social, and app.
- [ ] UI consistency held across every screen.
- [ ] Website and social imagery aligned with the approved brand system.
- [ ] Every design brief complete and approved before work began.
- [ ] Every revision stayed within its original scope.
- [ ] Every asset filed in the central, version-controlled library.
- [ ] Design Audit passed for every active channel.
- [ ] Founder or authority approval obtained as the final gate.
