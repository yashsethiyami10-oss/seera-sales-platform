**Content — the customer should recognise:** 1. the Muv identity; 2. the product type; 3. the
variant or fragrance; 4. the most important approved information; 5. the required action or
usage context. Decorative effects come after recognition and comprehension.

> **DESIGN RULE** — If an effect makes the logo, product type, variant, or mandatory
> information harder to understand, it is not premium.

**Packaging makes several silent promises:** the product is what it claims to be; the company
has controlled its information; the pack belongs to the same brand as other Muv products; the
customer can understand how to use it; the physical item should match the image used to sell
it. Inconsistent spelling, distorted bottles, incorrect labels, unclear variants, or missing
information weaken trust before the product is used.

## KO-CC-CH1-008 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IV, Ch.16, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 8-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CC-CH1-001 through 007.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 8 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Designing before confirming the real container. Stretching one label
across different sizes. Using category colour as the main identity. Sending a visual mockup as
a printer-ready file. Treating generated dimensions as production truth.

**Best Practices:** Start with a physical pack specification. Build a master hierarchy before
creating variants. Test labels on the actual bottle. Separate concept approval from production
approval. Maintain a controlled format matrix.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Confirm product and container.
- [ ] Confirm category, variant, and size.
- [ ] Use the current Muv identity.
- [ ] Establish hierarchy.
- [ ] Check format-specific constraints.
- [ ] Prepare production specifications.
- [ ] Review a physical-size proof.
- [ ] Record the approved version.

**Key Takeaways:** Design begins with recognition, customer trust, and product truth.
Packaging must operate as a coherent architecture, not as isolated artwork. Category, variant,
and size differences must remain controlled and recognisably Muv. Production feasibility is
part of design quality.

**Chapter Summary:** *"Packaging Architecture turns one Muv identity into a scalable physical
system. It protects recognition while allowing controlled differences across categories,
variants, and sizes."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: The Label Information System
defines exactly how product and company information should be organised"* — the sourced
