## KO-CC-CH4-001 — Role of a Mockup

- **Purpose:** Establish what a mockup is for and the Founder WARNING against over-trusting it.
- **Scope:** Role of a Mockup, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IV, Ch.19 (Role of a Mockup).
- **Outputs:** The mockup-purpose framing; the Founder WARNING.
- **Dependencies:** None (chapter-opening KO).
- **Relationships:** governs KO-CC-CH4-002 through 008.
- **Governance Rules:** **WARNING:** *"Never use a mockup as the only source for dimensions,
  product facts, or print production."*
- **Validation Rules:** The WARNING preserved verbatim, never softened.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** A mockup allows a concept, packaging idea, or digital layout to be visualised
before it is finalised. It helps the Founder and team evaluate an idea in a realistic context
without committing to full production. A mockup is a communication tool, not a production
record.

> **WARNING** — Never use a mockup as the only source for dimensions, product facts, or print
> production.

- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content — Figure 4.1 — 3D Visualization Standard:** concept brief → base model → material and
finish pass → label and identity application → lighting and environment → render → Founder or
authority review → approved for use / return for revision.

**Review at every stage:** does the 3D model match the actual product dimensions; is the label
artwork the current approved version; are materials, finishes, and colours accurate; is the cap,
pump, or closure represented correctly; is the render resolution sufficient for its intended use;
is the lighting realistic rather than artificially flattering in a misleading way; does the
render avoid implying features, size, or claims the physical product does not have; is the file
clearly labelled as a render, not a photograph, where the distinction matters; has the render
been compared against a real product reference.

## KO-CC-CH4-009 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part IV, Ch.19, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 9-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-CC-CH4-001 through 008.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established pattern; transitions to Chapter 5.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 9 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Treating a concept render as a final production reference. Using a mockup
to justify a customer-facing dimension or specification claim. Allowing an AI-generated render
to introduce features the physical product lacks. Letting social and website imagery drift from
the approved brand system over time. Skipping Founder or authority review before publishing a
generated asset. Mixing draft and final assets in the same customer-facing set without labelling.

**Best Practices:** Label every concept and draft asset clearly. Compare every 3D render against
a real product reference before approval. Maintain one shared Digital Design System across web,
social, and app surfaces. Review AI-generated assets against the same standard as any other
asset. Keep a record of what was generated, how, and by whom.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Mockup or render is not being used as the sole source of product facts.
- [ ] 3D model dimensions match the physical product.
- [ ] Label artwork on the render is the current approved version.
- [ ] Concept assets are clearly marked as concept, not final.
- [ ] Digital Design System components are used consistently.
- [ ] UI elements match across all screens and surfaces.
- [ ] Website and social imagery align with the approved brand system.
- [ ] AI-generated 3D/mockup/UI assets have passed the seven-step governance check.
- [ ] Founder or authority has reviewed any customer-facing generated asset.

**Key Takeaways:** A mockup communicates an idea; it does not certify a fact. Concept and final
assets must never be interchanged. One Digital Design System keeps web, social, and app
experiences coherent. AI can accelerate 3D, mockup, and UI production, but every generated
asset still answers to the same product-truth and brand standard as a photograph.

**Chapter Summary:** *"3D Visualization & Mockups exists to let ideas be explored and
communicated safely, without ever becoming an unverified substitute for the real, approved
product."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: Design Controls & Revision History defines how
design work is requested, revised, approved, and preserved over time"* — the sourced
justification for Chapter 5 following next.
