rejected. Instructions later included removal of ".a," revision of logo treatments, replacement
of old logos, addition of TM, and changing the relationship between the logo and supporting
language. These changes demonstrate that an idea's presence in historical work does not make it

## KO-BI-CH5-002 — Identity Decision States

- **Purpose:** Give any AI/governance system the formal seven-state taxonomy for classifying any
  identity decision's status — and retroactively ground informal "exploratory" language already
  used earlier in this domain.
- **Scope:** Identity Decision States, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 15 (Identity Decision States table).
- **Outputs:** The complete seven-row state table.
- **Dependencies:** None (foundational classification, applicable domain-wide).
- **Relationships:** **Retroactively grounds** `KO-BI-CH1-005`'s "affordable luxury from India"
  (informally called exploratory) and `KO-BI-CH4-002`'s category-colour list (informally called
  exploratory, not final) — both now formally classifiable as **Exploratory** per this table's
  own definition ("Being compared or visualised... Test without standardising"). This is a
  real connection the source's own chapter ordering created (Chapters 11 and 14 use the term
  before Chapter 15 formally defines it) — noted here, not retroactively edited into earlier
  chapters.
- **Governance Rules:** Every identity fact recorded anywhere in this domain should, in
  principle, be classifiable against this table — a fact with no stated state should not be
  assumed Approved by default.
- **Validation Rules:** All seven rows, three columns, exact match required.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

| State | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed | Suggested but not approved | Evaluate only |
| Exploratory | Being compared or visualised | Test without standardising |
| Qualified | Partly accepted; details remain open | Apply only settled elements |
| Approved | Explicitly chosen | Implement within scope |
| Deferred | Preserved for later decision | Do not activate |
| Rejected | Stopped | Keep outside active use |
| Superseded | Replaced by a later approved state | Preserve historically |

## KO-BI-CH5-003 — Internal Brand Governance

- **Purpose:** Give the authority structure, role responsibilities, and approval flow for any
  identity decision.
- **Scope:** §19 Internal Brand Governance, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 15, §19.
- **Outputs:** The Authority statement; the seven-role Brand Custody table; Figure 5.1's approval
  path; the eight required decision-record fields.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH1-001.
- **Relationships:** Directly grounds who is authorized to apply every rule established in
  Chapters 1–4 (e.g., who may approve a logo replacement per `KO-BI-CH4-008`'s Identity Change
  Control).
- **Governance Rules:** *"The Founder remains the final authority for Founder philosophy and
  major identity decisions. Teams may apply approved standards but cannot silently create new
  permanent identity rules."*
- **Validation Rules:** All seven Brand Custody roles must be preserved; the eight-field decision
  record list must never be shortened.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Authority:** The Founder remains the final authority for Founder philosophy and major identity
decisions. Teams may apply approved standards but cannot silently create new permanent identity
rules.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Founder | Final identity authority |
| Brand custodian | Maintains approved standards and master assets |
| Designer or agency | Creates within the approved system |
| Product and packaging team | Preserves identity while applying product truth |
| Digital team | Implements consistent reusable components |
| Marketplace team | Applies accurate templates and claims |
| Reviewer | Verifies consistency and decision status |

**Figure 5.1 — Brand Approval Path:** Brief → authority check → concept → application test →
Founder review where required → approval → master update → rollout → audit.

**Every identity decision should record:** what changed; why it changed; what remains fixed;
where it applies; approval state; effective version; old assets to retire; dependent systems to
update.

## KO-BI-CH5-004 — Brand Protection Rules

- **Purpose:** Give the complete, consolidated protection rule set spanning master assets,
  terminology, visuals, messaging, and channels — explicitly cross-referenced against the
  earlier chapters it restates in consolidated form.
- **Scope:** §20 Brand Protection Rules, complete, all five sub-categories.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 15, §20.
- **Outputs:** Five rule sub-categories; the Warning callout.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH1-002 (Founder philosophy), KO-BI-CH1-006/KO-BI-CH2-002 (unsupported
  claims), KO-BI-CH3-001/005 (pronunciation, Keep Muving spelling), KO-BI-CH5-001 (Muv.a).
- **Relationships:** **Terminology Protection** and **Message Protection** substantially restate
  rules already established in the dependencies listed above — recorded here as the source's own
  real, distinct consolidated formulation (a genuine chapter section, not invented), with the
  overlap made explicit rather than silently duplicated. **Master Asset Protection**, **Visual
  Protection**, and **Channel Protection** are largely new consolidation, extending
  `KO-BI-CH2-004` (Core Logo Rules) and `KO-BI-CH4-004`/`005` (Photography/Packaging).
- **Governance Rules:** *"Small uncontrolled differences become a second identity when repeated
  across enough touchpoints"* (Warning) — the single most important cross-cutting risk statement
  in this entire domain.
- **Validation Rules:** All five sub-categories and every rule within each must be preserved
  together; the Warning must never be dropped when this KO is cited.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Master Asset Protection:** Maintain one authoritative source for active brand assets. Restrict
editing of master logos. Use export files appropriate to the medium. Do not recreate master
assets from screenshots.

**Terminology Protection:** Write Muv according to the approved customer-facing form. Pronounce
it "move." Preserve Keep Muving spelling. Use trademark symbols only in the approved manner. Do
not reintroduce rejected naming such as Muv.a.

**Visual Protection:** Do not distort the logo. Do not alter product labels without instruction.
Do not use inconsistent category treatments. Do not allow generated imagery to change product
truth. Do not combine old and new active identity.

**Message Protection:** Do not invent Founder philosophy. Do not invent product claims. Do not
promise guarantees without operating authority. Do not use Keep Muving as filler. Do not confuse
historical language with active messaging.

**Channel Protection:** Update owned digital components from master assets. Audit marketplace
listing sets. Control vendor and agency files. Train staff and partners in pronunciation.
Preserve approved files for future franchise use.

**Warning:** Small uncontrolled differences become a second identity when repeated across enough
touchpoints.

## KO-BI-CH5-005 — Brand Exception Process

- **Purpose:** Give the exact procedure for handling a case where a standard genuinely cannot be
  followed, without letting exceptions silently become new standards.
- **Scope:** Brand Exception Process, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 15 (Brand Exception Process).
- **Outputs:** The 7-step process.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH5-003 (Approval Path — an exception still routes through the same
  authority structure).
- **Relationships:** Step 7 directly guards against the same "silent drift" risk named in
  `KO-BI-CH5-004`'s Warning.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Do not convert a one-time exception into a template"* (Step 7) — the
  binding constraint any future domain must respect when it encounters a case this domain's rules
  don't cleanly cover.
- **Validation Rules:** All 7 steps in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** When a standard cannot be followed: (1) state the exact constraint; (2) identify the
protected principle; (3) propose the smallest exception; (4) show the exception in real
application; (5) obtain the required approval; (6) record whether the exception is one-time or
permanent; (7) do not convert a one-time exception into a template.

## KO-BI-CH5-007 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve this final chapter's closing governance content, matching the established
  pattern from Chapters 1–4.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 15, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** A consolidated do/don't reference plus the chapter's own transition line (which is
  also this domain's own transition out, into Part IV — an out-of-scope volume).
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH5-001 through 006.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established Chapter-Governance-Summary pattern; this is the last
  instance of that pattern for Domain 1.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** Must not add an item not present in source.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Treating an old file as current because it is easy to find. Allowing a
vendor to create a new logo version. Failing to update templates after approval. Applying a
one-time exception across the system. Removing decision history and later repeating rejected
work.

**Best Practices:** Version every major identity release. Retain historical files outside
active-use folders. Audit all dependent channels after change. Train people in both rules and
reasons. Treat approval and rollout as separate stages.

**Chapter Summary:** *"Brand protection is the discipline of keeping one identity intact across
people, tools, channels, and time. Muv's history must remain preserved, but only the latest
approved state may govern active expression."* Source's own transition line: *"Next: Volume IV
converts identity into the detailed MUV Design System™ for packaging, labels, assets, and
production"* — confirming Part III (this domain's real source) ends here; Part IV is explicitly
out of Domain 1's scope, never pulled in.
