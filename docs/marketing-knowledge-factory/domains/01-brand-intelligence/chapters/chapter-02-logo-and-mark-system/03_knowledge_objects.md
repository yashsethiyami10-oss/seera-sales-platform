## KO-BI-CH2-001 — Customer Promise

- **Name:** Customer Promise
- **Purpose:** Give any AI system the one canonical, four-layer customer promise, distinct from
  (and never substitutable for) a product-specific guarantee.
- **Scope:** §5 Customer Promise, in full.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 12, §5 (lines 1924–1943).
- **Outputs:** The Foundation Customer Promise statement, the Promise Layer table, and the
  governing constraint on turning it into a measurable guarantee.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH1-001 (Brand Identity Foundation), KO-BI-CH1-003 (Mission).
- **Relationships:** Feeds KO-BI-CH2-002 (Brand Personality — both express "Caring").
- **Governance Rules:** Per the source's own Decision Point: never turn an emotional promise into
  a measurable product guarantee unless separately approved and fulfillable.
- **Validation Rules:** Table must reproduce all four rows exactly; the Decision Point callout
  must be preserved, not dropped.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

The customer promise is not a guarantee card, slogan, or claim created for one asset — it is the
consistent expectation produced by repeated brand behaviour.

**Foundation Customer Promise:** *"Muv aims to help customers feel clean, cared for, and ready to
Keep Muving through useful products and a clear, premium experience."* This promise does not
replace product-specific guarantees — any guarantee, performance claim, ingredient claim, health
claim, or refund commitment requires its own approved operational basis.

| Promise Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Functional | The product should perform its approved purpose |
| Emotional | The experience should feel caring and confidence-building |
| Brand | The expression should be consistent and recognisably Muv |
| Operational | The company should communicate honestly and correct problems |

**Decision Point (governance):** Never turn an emotional promise into a measurable product
guarantee unless the guarantee has been separately approved and can be fulfilled.

| Trait | Meaning | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Premium | Refined, deliberate, coherent | Flashy excess |
| Progressive | Forward-moving and improvement-oriented | Restlessness without direction |

## KO-BI-CH2-003 — Visual Identity Principles

- **Purpose:** Give any AI/design-adjacent system the five governing principles for how the Muv
  identity should be visually applied.
- **Scope:** §10 Visual Identity Principles, all five, plus Figure 2.1.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 12, §10 (lines 1960–1984).
- **Outputs:** Five named principles with exact descriptive text; the Visual Identity Hierarchy.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH1-001 (Brand Identity Foundation).
- **Relationships:** Forward relationship to Domain 1 Chapter 4 (Identity Governance — Typography/
  Colour/Photography all operationalize these five principles at a more granular level).
- **Governance Rules:** "Truthful Application" directly forbids altering the bottle/label/size/
  height without authorization — a real constraint any future Creative/Content Intelligence
  domain must inherit, not just Brand Intelligence.
- **Validation Rules:** All five principles must appear with their exact heading text; none may
  be paraphrased into a different name.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

- **Recognition before Decoration** — The customer must identify Muv before noticing secondary
  effects.
- **One Central Identity** — Product categories may use controlled supporting treatments, but
  they must not create separate versions of the brand.
- **Premium through Control** — Spacing, proportion, clarity, imagery, and consistency create
  premium character more reliably than effects.
- **Movement with Purpose** — Directional elements should reinforce movement without making the
  mark or layout busy.
- **Truthful Application** — Product imagery and packaging representation should remain
  realistic. Do not alter the bottle, label, size, height, or approved product appearance without
  authorization.

**Figure 2.1 — Visual Identity Hierarchy:** Muv identity → product/category truth → primary
message → supporting information → action.

## KO-BI-CH2-004 — Logo Usage: Core Requirements & Core Logo Rules

- **Purpose:** Give any AI/design-adjacent system the non-negotiable rules governing logo
  reproduction.
- **Scope:** §11's introductory "recurring requirements" list and the ten "Core Logo Rules."
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 12, §11 (lines 1986–2037, opening portion).
- **Outputs:** Seven recurring requirements; ten numbered Core Logo Rules, verbatim.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH2-003 (Visual Identity Principles — "Recognition before Decoration"
  and "One Central Identity" are the principles these rules operationalize).
- **Relationships:** Feeds KO-BI-CH2-006 (Logo Approval Checklist directly verifies compliance
  with these rules).
- **Governance Rules:** Rule 10 ("Do not use AI-generated approximations as master artwork") is
  directly self-referential for this very Knowledge Factory — any future Creative Intelligence
  domain generating AI logo variations must treat this as an absolute constraint, not a
  style preference.
- **Validation Rules:** All ten Core Logo Rules must be reproduced in their original numbered
  order; none merged, split, or paraphrased.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

The Founder's logo development included several recurring requirements: use a clear Muv identity;
maintain approved casing; apply the TM mark where approved; remove or retain supporting phrases
only according to the current asset decision; keep the logo visible in advertising and video; do
not distort the product or label when creating supporting images; replace old logos with the new
approved logo through controlled revision.

**Core Logo Rules:**
1. Use only an approved master logo.
2. Do not redraw, stretch, compress, rotate, or reconstruct it.
3. Preserve its proportions.
4. Do not add effects that reduce clarity.
5. Do not change the arrow or letter relationship without approval.
6. Use the approved trademark treatment.
7. Maintain sufficient separation from competing elements.
8. Use a background that preserves legibility.
9. Do not place an old and new logo within the same active identity system.
10. Do not use AI-generated approximations as master artwork.

## KO-BI-CH2-005 — Logo Placement

- **Purpose:** Give guidance on logo placement per application type, while correctly preserving
  the source's own caveat that this is pattern guidance, not universal law.
- **Scope:** §11's "Logo Placement" subsection.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part III, Chapter 12, §11 (lines ~2015–2030).
- **Outputs:** Five-row placement table; the Warning callout preserved intact.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH2-004.
- **Relationships:** Forward relationship to Domain 5 (Channel Intelligence, not yet started) —
  this table is directly relevant to per-channel logo application once that domain exists.
- **Governance Rules:** The Warning callout is load-bearing: **"A familiar placement pattern may
  guide templates, but a permanent universal placement rule requires formal approval and testing
  across formats."** This must never be dropped when this KO is reused/cited elsewhere — omitting
  it would misrepresent guidance as binding law.
- **Validation Rules:** The Warning callout must always accompany the table; the table must never
  be cited without it.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

Repeated advertising directions placed the logo at the top-right throughout video content. This
supports a useful consistency pattern for certain promotional formats, but it is not a universal
placement law for every medium.

| Application | Placement Principle |
|---|---|
| Video advertising | Maintain stable, visible placement where approved |
| Product image | Keep the product and its real label primary |
| Website | Use the logo consistently in navigation and controlled brand areas |
| Marketplace | Preserve recognition without obscuring the product |
| Documents | Use a disciplined header or cover system |

**Warning:** A familiar placement pattern may guide templates, but a permanent universal
placement rule requires formal approval and testing across formats.

- **Outputs:** A consolidated do/don't reference plus the chapter's own summary/transition line.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH2-001 through 006.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors `KO-BI-CH1-007`'s structural role for this chapter.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented — structural carry-over only.
- **Validation Rules:** Must not add an item not present in source; must not invent an "Action
  Checklist" this chapter does not have.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Recreating the logo from memory. Adding or removing supporting language
inconsistently. Altering the logo to fit a poor layout. Treating a generated visual as the master
identity. Changing the product label while creating marketplace imagery.

**Best Practices:** Maintain one controlled master-logo source. Lock logo components in
templates. Review logo use at final output size. Separate category colour from core logo
integrity. Preserve old versions historically but remove them from active use.

**Chapter Summary:** *"The Muv mark must create recognition through clarity and control. The logo
is not a flexible decoration; it is a protected identifier used consistently across touchpoints
while product and category variation remain subordinate."* Source's own transition line: *"Next:
Language, pronunciation, and messaging ensure the brand sounds as consistent as it looks"* — the
real, sourced justification for why Chapter 3 (Language, Pronunciation & Tagline) follows next in
this domain's frozen chapter sequence.
