- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.

**Content:** Technology creates value only when it improves a real business or customer
outcome. **Its role may include:** making information accessible; reducing avoidable manual
effort; creating consistent execution; connecting functions and partners; improving customer
experience; protecting business continuity; converting evidence into better decisions;
preserving organizational knowledge; and enabling controlled scale.

Buying software is not the same as building capability. Capability requires a fit between
purpose, process, people, data, technology, controls, and learning.

- **Purpose:** Give the seven-element digital-first design standard and the not-digital-only
  clarification.
- **Scope:** Digital-First Thinking, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part XII, Ch.58 (Digital-First Thinking).
- **Outputs:** Seven design elements; the not-digital-only clarification.
- **Dependencies:** KO-DM-CH6-001.
- **Relationships:** feeds KO-DM-CH6-003.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Digital-first does not mean digital-only."*
- **Validation Rules:** All seven elements preserved; the clarification never dropped.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Digital-first does not mean digital-only. It means that Muv should design
information and workflows so they can be: consistently created; securely accessed;
responsibly reused; measured where useful; connected across approved systems; maintained
through change; and understood by the people accountable for them. The correct experience may
still include a human conversation, physical product, printed label, retail interaction, or
manual exception. Digital-first thinking connects those experiences instead of treating them as
separate worlds.

## KO-DM-CH6-004 — Technology Principles

- **Purpose:** Preserve the twelve technology principles.
- **Scope:** Technology Principles, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part XII, Ch.58 (Technology Principles).
- **Outputs:** Twelve named principles.
- **Dependencies:** KO-DM-CH6-003.
- **Relationships:** feeds KO-DM-CH6-005.
- **Governance Rules:** None new — twelve governing principles in their own right.
- **Validation Rules:** All twelve principles preserved in exact order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content — Muv technology should be:** 1. Purpose-led — tied to a defined outcome. 2.
Customer-aware — designed around real journeys and needs. 3. Simple enough to govern —
complexity must earn its place. 4. Secure and private by design — protection begins before
launch. 5. Accessible — usable by intended people across relevant contexts. 6. Reliable —
monitored and supported in proportion to importance. 7. Interoperable — able to exchange
approved information without uncontrolled duplication. 8. Documented — ownership, logic,
dependencies, and changes remain visible. 9. Evidence-driven — performance is reviewed through
appropriate measures. 10. Human-accountable — material decisions retain named ownership. 11.
Adaptable — systems can evolve without destroying continuity. 12. Reversible where possible —
uncertain choices should avoid unnecessary lock-in.

- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content — every material technology system requires:**

| Ownership Area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Business owner | Defines outcome, priority, process, and acceptable service |
| System owner | Maintains the system lifecycle and operating accountability |
| Data owner | Authorizes and governs defined data |
| Technical owner | Manages architecture, configuration, integration, and technical health |
| Security/privacy owner | Reviews protection requirements and risk |
| Process owner | Ensures technology supports the approved workflow |
| Vendor owner | Manages the external relationship and obligations |
| User owner | Ensures access, capability, adoption, and feedback |

One person may hold multiple roles in an early-stage environment, but the responsibilities must
remain explicit.
