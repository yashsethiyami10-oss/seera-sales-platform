**Content:**

> **KEY INSIGHT** — The website is not an isolated visual asset. It is the customer-facing
> layer of a wider operating system connecting brand, products, orders, content, communication,
> and company control.

Muv's digital journey began with practical questions: Where should the website be built? Can
customers place orders? Can payment links be integrated? How will existing product photographs
be used? How can the website feel premium? How will work continue when tools, sessions, or
technical limits interrupt progress? These questions gradually expanded the website from a
page-building exercise into a platform problem.

**The digital foundation must serve two realities at once:** 1. The customer needs a clear,
trustworthy, premium experience. 2. The company needs an editable, maintainable, verifiable
system behind it.

## KO-DM-CH1-004 — Foundation Principles

- **Purpose:** Preserve the five foundation principles governing platform design discipline.
- **Scope:** Foundation Principles, complete (One System Multiple Journeys, Real Capability
  over Visual Simulation, Mobile Is a Primary View, Editable without Identity Drift,
  Step-by-Step without Loss of System Context).
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part II, Ch.6 (Foundation Principles).
- **Outputs:** Five named principles with their governing statements.
- **Dependencies:** KO-DM-CH1-003.
- **Relationships:** feeds KO-DM-CH1-005.
- **Governance Rules:** *"A button, form, login option, or order status must not imply a
  functioning capability unless the supporting workflow exists."* *"Content must be manageable,
  but editing freedom cannot permit accidental changes to protected brand assets, product
  facts, or approved language."*
- **Validation Rules:** All five principles preserved with their exact governing statements.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**One System, Multiple Journeys** — The owned platform may serve retail customers, business
enquiries, distributors, institutions, and franchise prospects. Their journeys differ, but the
platform should not become a collection of disconnected websites.

**Real Capability over Visual Simulation** — A button, form, login option, or order status must
not imply a functioning capability unless the supporting workflow exists.

**Mobile Is a Primary View** — The Founder repeatedly needed to review the mobile experience.
Mobile cannot be treated as a late reduction of the desktop design.

**Editable without Identity Drift** — Content must be manageable, but editing freedom cannot
permit accidental changes to protected brand assets, product facts, or approved language.

**Step-by-Step without Loss of System Context** — Each implementation step must remain
connected to the full platform plan. Small tasks should not erase dependencies or future
requirements.

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Beginning with visual effects before system scope is clear. Treating
desktop output as proof of mobile quality. Building mock interactions without a real operating
path. Allowing content editing to change protected knowledge. Losing implementation context
when work moves between tools or sessions.

**Best Practices:** Keep a current system checklist. Explain each technical step in operational
language. Record what is complete, open, rejected, or deferred. Verify the real journey, not
only the visual screen. Preserve a clear restart point after every stage.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] State the platform objective.
- [ ] Identify every intended user journey.
- [ ] Confirm which capabilities are real and which remain planned.
- [ ] Map each visible element to its data or operating source.
- [ ] Check mobile and desktop.
- [ ] Record the next resumable step.
