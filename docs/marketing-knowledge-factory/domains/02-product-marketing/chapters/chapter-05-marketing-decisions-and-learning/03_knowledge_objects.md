  policy without explicit approval — this domain's clearest epistemic-humility statement.

- **Governance Rules:** *"A campaign concept, generated script, market list, pricing estimate, or
  high-performing asset does not become Founder doctrine or permanent marketing policy without
  explicit evidence and approval."*
- **Validation Rules:** The Marketing Rule must never be dropped when any campaign-artifact
  content from this domain is cited as if it were settled policy.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

Rejected discussions remain preserved where reasons were not explicit.

**Marketing Rule:** A campaign concept, generated script, market list, pricing estimate, or
high-performing asset does not become Founder doctrine or permanent marketing policy without
explicit evidence and approval.

- **Purpose:** Give the six root measurement questions and the nine-level hierarchy connecting
  input to learning.
- **Scope:** Marketing Measurement Philosophy (Figure 5.1), Measurement Hierarchy.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.35 (Marketing Measurement Philosophy,
  Measurement Hierarchy).
- **Outputs:** Six root questions; Figure 5.1; nine-row hierarchy table.

- **Relationships:** Governs KO-PM-CH5-003 through 007 (all specific metrics/evaluations trace
  back to this hierarchy).
- **Governance Rules:** None new — a measurement framework, not a claim rule.
- **Validation Rules:** Figure 5.1's nine-stage sequence and the nine-row hierarchy table both

- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Measurement should answer: Did we reach the intended people? Did they understand
the intended value? Did they take the intended action? Was the action economically and
operationally useful? Did the product experience support retention and trust? What did we learn?

**Figure 5.1 — Marketing Measurement Framework:** Input → Delivery → Attention → Understanding →
Action → Customer quality → Retention → Brand effect → Learning.

| Level | Example questions |
|---|---|
| Inputs | What time, media, content, creator, and budget were used? |
| Delivery | Was the campaign served or published as planned? |
| Attention | Did relevant people view, stop, or engage? |
| Understanding | Did they remember and understand the message? |
| Action | Did they visit, inquire, add, buy, or subscribe? |
| Quality | Were customers relevant, satisfied, and supportable? |
| Economics | Did value justify the complete cost? |
| Retention | Did customers repeat, remain, or advocate? |
| Brand health | Did awareness, consideration, trust, or consistency improve? |

## KO-PM-CH5-004 — Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

- **Purpose:** Give the exact CAC formula and the seven components that must be defined before
  it can be calculated meaningfully.
- **Scope:** Customer Acquisition Cost, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.35 (Customer Acquisition Cost).
- **Outputs:** The CAC formula; seven required definitional components; the misleading-low-CAC
  warning.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH5-003.
- **Relationships:** Directly implements `KO-PM-CH1-005`'s Trust Factors principle (a cheap
  acquisition number without quality context is exactly the kind of unsupported-seeming metric
  this domain repeatedly warns against).
- **Governance Rules:** *"A low CAC can be misleading if retention, margin, complaint, or
  fulfilment quality is weak."*
- **Validation Rules:** The formula must never be quoted without its seven required defined
  components — a bare CAC number without basis definition is meaningless per this KO's own
  content.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** CAC must use a defined basis: **CAC = Total included acquisition cost ÷ Number of
acquired customers meeting the defined rule.**

**Define:** period; channels; included media, agency, creator, content, discount, and technology
costs; new-customer definition; attribution method; cancellations, returns, and invalid orders;
tax and commercial treatment where relevant.

A low CAC can be misleading if retention, margin, complaint, or fulfilment quality is weak.

## KO-PM-CH5-005 — Return on Marketing Investment (ROMI)

- **Purpose:** Forbid the single most common measurement-fraud pattern — comparing gross revenue
  to partial media cost.
- **Scope:** Return on Marketing Investment, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.35 (Return on Marketing Investment).
- **Outputs:** Four possible analysis types; the prohibition rule.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH5-004.
- **Relationships:** None new.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Never compare gross revenue with partial media cost and label it
  complete return."*
- **Validation Rules:** Any ROMI figure cited must state what return and cost are included, per
  the chapter's opening instruction.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** ROMI must state what return and cost are included. Possible analysis: incremental
contribution versus marketing cost; customer lifetime contribution versus acquisition cost;
campaign revenue with limitations; brand or research outcome where direct revenue attribution is
inappropriate. Never compare gross revenue with partial media cost and label it complete return.

## KO-PM-CH5-008 — Marketing Experimentation

- **Purpose:** Give the experiment-record structure and the guardrail-metrics discipline
  preventing "improve one metric, harm another" outcomes.
- **Scope:** Marketing Experimentation (Experiment Record, Guardrail Metrics), complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.35 (Marketing Experimentation, Guardrail
  Metrics).
- **Outputs:** Twelve experiment-record fields; eight possible test variables; nine guardrail
  categories.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH5-007.
- **Relationships:** Directly extends `KO-PM-CH2-009` (Truth-Meaning-Attention Gate) — an
  experiment must never sacrifice truth/meaning for an attention-metric win, exactly what
  guardrails exist to catch.
- **Governance Rules:** *"An experiment changes a defined variable to reduce uncertainty... Do
  not change everything and call the result a test."*
- **Validation Rules:** All twelve record fields and all nine guardrail categories preserved.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** An experiment changes a defined variable to reduce uncertainty.

**Experiment Record fields:** hypothesis; objective; audience; baseline/control; variable;
protected elements; sample and duration; primary metric; guardrail metrics; result; limitation;
decision.

**Possible variables include:** message; proof; creative format; audience; placement; landing
sequence; timing; offer. Do not change everything and call the result a test.

**Guardrail Metrics** — an experiment can improve one metric while harming another. Guardrails
may include: claim accuracy; brand consistency; customer quality; return/cancellation; complaint
rate; margin; frequency/fatigue; unsubscribe; reputation.

## KO-PM-CH5-015 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve this final chapter's closing governance content, matching the domain's
  established pattern.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.35, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 11-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary; the domain's own closing transition line (to Volume VIII, out of scope).
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH5-001 through 014.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established Chapter-Governance-Summary pattern; this is the last
  instance of that pattern for Domain 2.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 11 checklist items in original order (one more than most prior
  chapters — matching this chapter's own real, larger scope).
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Selecting metrics after results arrive. Optimising click cost instead of
customer quality. Claiming causal impact from weak attribution. Comparing campaigns with
different cost bases. Allowing high-performing off-brand work to become a standard. Saving
reports without decisions. Repeating experiments because learning was not archived.

**Best Practices:** Set objectives and primary metrics before launch. Use guardrails. Review
cohorts, not only totals. Combine quantitative data with customer questions and feedback.
Preserve failed tests and rejected directions. Turn every report into a documented decision or
open question. Train future leaders in truth, measurement, and brand discipline together.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Objective, audience, baseline, and metrics defined.
- [ ] CAC and ROMI bases documented.
- [ ] Brand-health measures extend beyond social counts.
- [ ] Campaign review covers message, journey, customer quality, and economics.
- [ ] Experiments isolate meaningful variables.
- [ ] Guardrails protect brand, claims, and customer experience.
- [ ] Decisions link to evidence and authority.
- [ ] Rejected work remains traceable.
- [ ] Assets, rights, versions, and approvals controlled.
- [ ] AI contribution and human review recorded.
- [ ] Learning enters the next campaign and leadership review.

**Key Takeaways:** Marketing measurement exists to improve decisions, not decorate reports.
Acquisition economics must include customer quality and a defined cost basis. Experiments need
hypotheses, controls, guardrails, and documented outcomes. Future marketing leadership must
protect truth and trust while building growth.
