## KO-PM-CH1-001 — Marketing Philosophy

- **Name:** Marketing Philosophy
- **Purpose:** Establish the non-negotiable bridge model — marketing connects, never alters,
  brand and product truth.
- **Scope:** Marketing Philosophy, Promotion vs. Value Communication, Brand/Product/Customer/
  Market relationship table.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31 (lines 6714–6759).
- **Outputs:** Figure 1.1 bridge model; Promotion-vs-Value table; the 6-element relationship
  table.
- **Dependencies:** `KO-BI-CH1-001` (Brand identity — "what Muv is," never alterable by
  marketing).
- **Relationships:** Governs every later chapter in this domain — the foundational bridge model.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Marketing is the bridge. It must not alter either side: it cannot
  change what Muv is; it cannot change what the product can prove."* Promotion "can amplify a
  message. It cannot substitute for value."
- **Validation Rules:** The Promotion-vs-Value table's four rows must never be presented as
  interchangeable — promotion is explicitly subordinate to value communication.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Muv does not sell only products — it builds relationships through trust, usefulness,
experience, consistency, emotional connection.

**Figure 1.1 — Muv Marketing Philosophy:** Founder intent → Brand identity → Product truth →
Customer problem → Useful message → Trusted experience → Learning → Responsible growth.

| Promotion | Value communication |
|---|---|
| Seeks an immediate response | Helps the customer understand relevance |
| May use an offer, urgency, or activation | Uses problem, benefit, proof, use, and trust |
| Is usually time-bound | Can strengthen long-term understanding |
| Can amplify a message | Must not manufacture the underlying truth |

**Founder Note:** The recovered marketing record moves repeatedly through launch, teaser, ad,
script, sales pitch, creator, and market-list questions. The permanent requirement is not one
campaign — it is the ability to build communication around a defined product, audience, place,
and objective.

| Element | Governing question |
|---|---|
| Brand | Who is speaking and what must remain recognisably Muv? |
| Product | What verified value exists? |
| Customer | For whom is that value relevant, and in what situation? |
| Market | Where and against what alternatives is the choice made? |
| Message | What should the customer understand now? |
| Experience | What happens after attention and purchase? |

Marketing fails when any one element is invented, missing, or disconnected.

## KO-PM-CH1-002 — Customer-First Thinking & Customer Needs

- **Purpose:** Give the eight diagnostic questions and the five-layer needs model that ground
  every campaign in real customer situations.
- **Scope:** Customer-First Thinking, Customer Needs.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31 (lines 6761–6788).
- **Outputs:** Eight diagnostic questions; five-row needs-layer table.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH1-001.
- **Relationships:** Feeds KO-PM-CH1-004 (Functional/Emotional Drivers).
- **Governance Rules:** *"An emotional message must remain anchored to product and experience
  truth"* — direct extension of Domain 1's Message Protection discipline.
- **Validation Rules:** All eight questions and all five need-layer rows preserved together.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Customer-first marketing does not mean agreeing with every preference — it means
beginning with the customer's real situation and respecting their intelligence.

**Ask:** What problem are they trying to solve? What outcome matters most? What do they
currently use? What confuses or worries them? What proof would reduce doubt? What language do
they naturally use? What effort, price, time, or risk do they perceive? What would make them
trust and continue?

| Need layer | Marketing implication |
|---|---|
| Functional | Explain the product's approved job and use |
| Practical | Explain convenience, dosage, format, access, or cost per use where verified |
| Emotional | Address confidence, care, relief, pride, freshness, or control without manipulation |
| Social | Understand household, peer, creator, professional, or community influence |
| Trust | Provide clarity, evidence, consistency, and support |

An emotional message must remain anchored to product and experience truth.

## KO-PM-CH1-006 — Customer Journey Map & Feedback as Learning

- **Purpose:** Give the eight-stage journey map and the honest boundary on what marketing may
  conclude from customer feedback.
- **Scope:** Customer Journey, Customer Feedback as Learning.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31 (lines 6839–6871).
- **Outputs:** Figure 1.3, eight-stage table; ten feedback categories; the Volume V/VI deferral.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH1-001.
- **Relationships:** Feeds every later chapter (journey stage is the organizing axis for
  Campaign, Content, and Creator strategy).
- **Governance Rules:** *"Marketing can identify patterns, but technical causes and product
  changes remain governed by Volume V and Volume VI"* — explicit domain-boundary deferral, never
  resolved by this domain.
- **Validation Rules:** All eight journey stages in exact order; the Volume V/VI deferral must
  always accompany any feedback-classification content.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** **Figure 1.3 — Customer Journey Map:**

| Stage | Customer question | Marketing task |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Is there a problem or better way? | Make the situation recognisable |
| Aware | Does this matter to me? | Explain relevance |
| Considering | Why this product and Muv? | Communicate value, proof, and difference |
| Evaluating | Can I trust it? | Clarify claims, use, price, reviews, and support |
| Buying | Is the choice easy and safe? | Remove avoidable friction |
| Using | Am I getting the expected experience? | Support correct use and expectation |
| Repeating | Is it worth continuing? | Reinforce value and reliability |
| Advocating | Should I recommend it? | Enable honest sharing and community |

Marketing does not end at purchase — post-purchase truth determines whether acquisition becomes
relationship.

**Customer Feedback as Learning — classify feedback:** problem or unmet need; question;
objection; usability difficulty; product performance observation; preference; praise; complaint;
misunderstanding; new use request. **Marketing can identify patterns, but technical causes and
product changes remain governed by Volume V and Volume VI.**

## KO-PM-CH1-007 — Launch System

- **Purpose:** Give the exact 11-step launch sequence and its Pre-Launch/Launch/Sustain phase
  breakdown — two distinct structures, never merged.
- **Scope:** Launch Architecture (11-step Figure 1.4), Pre-Launch/Launch/Sustain.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31 (lines 6873–6933).
- **Outputs:** 11-step sequence; three 6-item phase lists.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH1-006 (journey stages inform launch phase design).
- **Relationships:** Feeds KO-PM-CH1-008 (Launch Readiness — the gate before step 8).
- **Governance Rules:** *"A launch is a coordinated market introduction, not one post."*
- **Validation Rules:** The 11 steps and the 3-phase breakdown must be presented as two distinct
  structures (per source) — never collapsed into "steps 8-10 = the three phases," which would
  misrepresent the source.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** A launch is a coordinated market introduction, not one post.

**Figure 1.4 — Muv Launch System:** (1) Confirm product and launch state. (2) Define audience,
problem, and market. (3) Define objective. (4) Build approved value and claim foundation. (5)
Build message architecture. (6) Create channel and asset plan. (7) Prepare sales, availability,
support, and measurement. (8) Run pre-launch. (9) Activate launch. (10) Sustain education and
proof. (11) Review performance and customer learning.

**Pre-Launch:** establish relevance; create informed curiosity; introduce the customer problem;
prepare proof and education; build waiting or contact mechanisms; test message comprehension.

**Launch:** reveal the product clearly; state approved value; explain use and availability;
coordinate channels; support questions and conversion; monitor customer response.

**Sustain:** deepen education; demonstrate use; answer objections; collect credible proof and
feedback; support repeat and referral; improve the campaign.

## KO-PM-CH1-009 — Long-Term Brand Building & Decision Points

- **Purpose:** Give the compounding-trust principle and the five pre-launch go/hold questions.
- **Scope:** Long-Term Brand Building, Decision Points.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31 (lines 6935–6958).
- **Outputs:** Six compounding behaviours; five decision questions; the hold condition.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH1-001, KO-PM-CH1-008.
- **Relationships:** Decision Points functions as the final gate before `KO-PM-CH1-007`'s step 9
  (Activate launch).
- **Governance Rules:** *"Hold the launch if the campaign is ready but product, evidence,
  inventory, or customer support is not."* — campaign readiness alone is never sufficient.
- **Validation Rules:** The hold condition must always accompany the five decision questions.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Long-term marketing compounds when Muv repeatedly: communicates recognisable
principles; solves relevant problems; explains products honestly; maintains consistent identity;
delivers the promised experience; remembers customer learning. Short-term attention that weakens
trust creates negative compounding.

**Decision Points, before launch:** Is the product truly ready for the promoted claim and
market? Is availability reliable? Does the audience understand the message? Are brand, design,
sales, and service aligned? Can performance be measured? **Hold the launch if the campaign is
ready but product, evidence, inventory, or customer support is not.**

## KO-PM-CH1-010 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content, establishing the pattern this
  domain's remaining chapters will follow.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.31, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 9-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary; transition line.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH1-001 through 009.
- **Relationships:** Establishes this domain's own Chapter-Governance-Summary pattern (Domain 2's
  chapters additionally carry "Key Takeaways," a subsection Domain 1's chapters did not have —
  noted as a real structural difference between the two source Parts, not an inconsistency).
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 9 checklist items in original order; must not add an item not present
  in source.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Starting with a post rather than a launch objective. Choosing a broad
audience to avoid making a decision. Using emotional language without functional substance.
Promoting before supply and service are ready. Treating pre-launch curiosity as demand proof.
Ending the campaign at the first sale.

**Best Practices:** Begin with one customer and one problem. Build the message from approved
product truth. Create a launch readiness gate. Plan post-purchase communication before
acquisition. Record questions and objections from the first day. Review learning, not only
reach.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Product, claim, and market state verified.
- [ ] Audience and problem defined.
- [ ] Functional and emotional drivers documented.
- [ ] Customer journey mapped.
- [ ] Launch objective and primary message approved.
- [ ] Assets, channels, availability, sales, and service aligned.
- [ ] Pre-launch, launch, and sustain stages planned.
- [ ] Measurement and feedback systems ready.
- [ ] Hold conditions identified.

**Key Takeaways:** Marketing connects brand and product truth to a real customer situation.
Promotion amplifies value; it does not create it. Trust is built across the complete journey. A
launch is a coordinated system that continues after attention and purchase.

**Chapter Summary:** *"Launch Architecture establishes marketing as customer understanding,
truthful value communication, coordinated readiness, and long-term trust. It turns a product
introduction into a complete learning and relationship system."* Source's own transition line:
*"Next: Campaign Concepts & Advertising converts the launch foundation into channel-aware,
claim-controlled creative strategy"* — the sourced justification for Chapter 2 following next.
