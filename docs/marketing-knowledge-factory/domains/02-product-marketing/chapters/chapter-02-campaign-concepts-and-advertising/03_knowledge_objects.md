## KO-PM-CH2-003 — Messaging Architecture & Brand Voice

- **Purpose:** Give the six-layer message model and the binding rule that Marketing applies,
  never owns, brand voice.
- **Scope:** Messaging Architecture (Figure 2.2), Brand Voice.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.32 (Messaging Architecture, Brand Voice).
- **Outputs:** Six-layer table; six voice-application qualities.
- **Dependencies:** KO-BI-CH3-003 (Tone of Voice, Domain 1, frozen), KO-BI-CH1-001.
- **Relationships:** **Direct cross-domain citation** — "Brand belief" layer is explicitly
  *"owned by Volume III"*; "Volume III owns voice. Marketing applies it" is quoted verbatim, not
  restated as new content.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Channel adaptation may change length, rhythm, and format. It must not
  change identity or truth."*
- **Validation Rules:** All six message layers preserved; the Brand Voice ownership statement
  never presented as if Marketing (Domain 2) originates voice rules.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** **Figure 2.2 — Brand Communication Framework:**

| Message layer | Role |
|---|---|
| Brand belief | The enduring Muv meaning owned by Volume III |
| Customer problem | The recognisable situation |
| Product promise | The approved outcome or value |
| Reason to believe | Evidence, product information, use, demonstration, or experience |
| Emotional meaning | Why the outcome matters personally |
| Action | The appropriate next step |

Every asset need not contain every layer. The campaign system must.

**Brand Voice:** Volume III owns voice. Marketing applies it. The application should remain:
clear rather than inflated; confident rather than unsupported; useful rather than empty; human
rather than mechanically generic; consistent rather than identical; respectful rather than
manipulative. Channel adaptation may change length, rhythm, and format — it must not change
identity or truth.

- **Governance Rules:** Every claim must connect to: Exact product version → Approved wording →
  Evidence → Conditions of use → Channel application. No claim skips a stage.
- **Validation Rules:** All seven claim-type rows preserved; the five-stage chain never
  shortened or reordered.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.

**Content:** Every claim must connect to: **Exact product version → Approved wording → Evidence

| Claim type | Control |
|---|---|
| Functional | Must match validated product performance |
| Ingredient | Must match actual approved formula and permissible communication |
| Comparative | Requires a defined, supportable comparison |
| Numeric | Requires exact calculation or test evidence |
| Time-bound | Requires relevant final-product evidence |
| Safety/suitability | Requires appropriate support and qualification |
| Testimonial/review | Must remain authentic and contextual |

## KO-PM-CH2-009 — Advertising Concept Evaluation (Truth–Meaning–Attention Gate)

- **Purpose:** Give the three-part, strictly-ordered gate every creative concept must pass.
- **Scope:** Advertising Concept Evaluation, complete.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.32 (Advertising Concept Evaluation).
- **Outputs:** The three-stage gate, in mandatory order.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH2-001, KO-PM-CH2-008.
- **Relationships:** The domain's central creative-approval mechanism — every future Creative
  Intelligence domain KO evaluating an ad concept should apply this gate.
- **Governance Rules:** *"Never reverse the sequence."* — Truth and Meaning must be confirmed
  before Attention is ever used as an approval criterion.
- **Validation Rules:** The three stages must always be presented in order (Truth → Meaning →
  Attention), never Attention-first.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:** Use the Truth–Meaning–Attention Gate: (1) **Truth** — Is every product, claim,
result, and representation accurate? (2) **Meaning** — Will the intended customer understand why
it matters? (3) **Attention** — Is the expression distinctive enough to be noticed? **Never
reverse the sequence.**

## KO-PM-CH2-014 — Chapter Governance Summary

- **Purpose:** Preserve the chapter's closing governance content.
- **Scope:** Common Mistakes, Best Practices, Action Checklist, Key Takeaways, Chapter Summary.
- **Inputs:** MUV Knowledge Library, Part VII, Ch.32, closing subsections.
- **Outputs:** Consolidated do/don't reference; 10-item Action Checklist; 4 Key Takeaways;
  Chapter Summary.
- **Dependencies:** KO-PM-CH2-001 through 013.
- **Relationships:** Mirrors the established Chapter-Governance-Summary pattern.
- **Governance Rules:** No new rule invented.
- **Validation Rules:** All 10 checklist items in original order.
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Founder Review Ready
- **Change History:** 2026-07-31 — created.
- **Evidence Classification:** Verified

**Content:**

**Common Mistakes:** Beginning with a slogan instead of an objective. Putting multiple primary
messages into one asset. Using a claim because a competitor uses it. Creating visual drama that
changes the product. Copying long website text into every channel. Treating marketplace images
as disconnected sales graphics. Measuring campaign quality by attention alone.

**Best Practices:** Approve the message before producing assets. Give each asset one role. Build
a proof library linked to claims. Review campaign assets together as a sequence. Adapt for
customer intent and channel format. Test comprehension before increasing media spend.

**Action Checklist:**
- [ ] Objective, audience, problem, and desired action clear.
- [ ] Product and offer verified.
- [ ] Primary message and proof approved.
- [ ] Claim evidence linked.
- [ ] Emotional meaning remains truthful.
- [ ] Creative concept passes Truth–Meaning–Attention.
- [ ] Channel roles and formats defined.
- [ ] Marketplace and retail assets preserve product truth.
- [ ] Sales, inventory, and service dependencies aligned.
- [ ] Measurement plan approved.

**Key Takeaways:** A campaign is an objective-led communication system, not a collection of
assets. Message architecture connects brand, customer problem, product promise, proof, and
action. Attention follows truth and meaning. Channel adaptation must preserve one product and
one brand reality.
